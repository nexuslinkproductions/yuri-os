a scalar potential for agent control · running in observe mode

A machine that refuses to make itself worse.

Every system that acts can drift. It accumulates uncertainty, lets its claims wander from its evidence, lowers its own standards one tolerable step at a time. This one scores every move it is about to make against a single number, and takes the move only if it does not raise that number.

from action to number

How an action becomes a number.

The model does not score itself. It acts, and the system measures the action. The same six steps run on every move it makes.

  1. 1
    A tool finishes. The moment an edit, a write, or a command completes, the system catches it holding three facts: which tool ran, what it touched, and whether it errored.
  2. 2
    The action becomes plain facts. Was this a write or a command. Did it succeed or fail. Does the path it touched fall inside a protected zone. Nothing is interpreted; the reading is a few booleans and a path.
  3. 3
    Only moves that matter are measured. Reads, navigation, and lookups change nothing, so they are dropped before any arithmetic. What passes through is work that changed or verified something, and the moments that carry weight: a failure, or a write into a protected zone.
  4. 4
    The facts move a small ledger. A success adds a verified-evidence credit. A failure is logged as a claim that did not hold, since the move implied success and the outcome was failure. A protected write adds a violation. The ledger before the action and the ledger after it are two distinct states.
  5. 5
    The factors score both states. Each factor multiplies one field of the ledger by its weight. Verified credit pulls the number down. A claim that did not hold pushes it up. A violation pushes it up by a hundred, past anything credit can cancel. Sum the factors before, sum them after, subtract.
  6. 6
    The sign decides. At or below zero the move did not make the system worse, so it runs. Above zero it did, so it is stopped. A violation alone forces the difference near a hundred, so it never runs.

The number stays auditable because it is built from what the action did, never from a sense of whether it looked right. You can point to the factor that moved and the fact behind it. Two moves run the same path below: one lands, one is stopped before it touches anything.

the running total

Thousands of moves later, the number keeps falling.

Every move adds its effect to a running total. Across the recorded window that total falls and holds there, as work lands and verifies faster than anything pushes the number up. The gate scores each move and records the verdict without stopping anything yet, so this curve is the shape of healthy work.

the factors

Eleven factors, and what each one charges for.

Each factor turns one property of a move into points. Most are penalties, for the ways work quietly degrades: claims drifting from the evidence, forecasts that miss, the same mistake repeating, evidence going stale. One is a credit, the only thing that earns slack, for work that has been verified. The gate reads the property off the move, multiplies it by the factor's weight, and the move either banks credit or pays a penalty.

The weights are policy, set by hand, not learned from data. Two of them act as vetoes rather than weights. A write into a protected zone, or a forecast outside the range a probability can take, each carries a penalty so large that no accumulated credit can buy it back. Those two are the hard floor under everything the system is allowed to do.

tuning

Every weight is yours to set.

None of these numbers are fixed. Raise the cost of a drifting claim, ease the penalty on a stale forecast, or move the line where a move gets rejected, and the next move the gate scores feels the change. The values shown are the settings in force right now.

the shape of the rule

Why a downhill-only rule comes to rest.

Picture every move the system could make as a point on a surface, where height is the number the gate would give it. The bright wells are the low points, the states the system is content to rest in. The short arrows are the downhill direction at each spot, the way the number drops fastest. The travelling points are moves finding their way across that surface.

On the left the rule is in force: take only downhill moves. Every point is drawn into a well and stays there, because from anywhere there is one way down and no way to climb. On the right the rule is off, and the same points keep circling, shoved around by local motion with no single number to settle them. Same machine, once with the rule and once without.

The same surface, drawn as its skeleton.

Strip the surface down to its low points and the walls between them. Each dot is a resting state. The height where two branches join is the wall a move would have to climb to cross from one to the other. The rule keeps the system dropping toward the deepest dot and never paying to climb back out.

when a move is refused

A refused move is held, not killed.

This is the part that matters most for anyone who is not an expert. When the gate scores a move above the line, the move does not run and it does not disappear. It is set aside in a contained space with the reason attached: the factor that pushed it over, and by how much. A write outside its lane, a forecast that cannot be true, a step that skips the evidence it should rest on.

From there it is the operator's call. Read the reason, then let the move through or keep it blocked. Nothing is lost either way, since the work is paused rather than thrown out, so the gate never costs you a move you actually wanted. Those decisions teach it. The moves you wave through and the ones you hold shape where the line sits, until the gate refuses what you would refuse and stops interrupting you over what you would have allowed. For someone still learning, that is the difference between a small warning and a mistake that is expensive to undo.

what it catches, and what it lets through

Run the gate over moves whose right answer is already settled and it sorts them cleanly. Four that should never run: a write outside its lane, a forecast claimed with false confidence, a forecast that cannot be true, a step that jumps the evidence under it. Each is held, with the factor that stopped it named. Two ordinary moves, a verified edit and a no-op, pass straight through.

Replayed across the whole stream of ordinary work it has watched, it holds back none of it. Catching the dangerous move without standing in the way of honest work is the only version worth handing the keys to.

the contained space

Before it ever holds your work, it is hardened on work that is not yours.

A refused move is only as safe as the place it is held. That place is a sealed copy of the system, seven layers deep, where the gate is pushed against the worst inputs anyone can write without a single live operation at risk. State stays inside the boundary, and only sanitized records ever leave it. The gate earns the right to hold a move here first, under pressure, long before it is trusted to hold one for you.

one rule, many readings

The same descent, read through five different doors.

"Accept only what does not raise the score" is not a trick for one domain. It is a single discipline that re-reads cleanly wherever a system must move through states without quietly degrading.
