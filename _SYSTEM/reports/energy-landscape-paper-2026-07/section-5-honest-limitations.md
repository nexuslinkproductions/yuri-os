# Section 5: Honest Limitations

The proposal as specified in Sections 3 and 4 carries five limitations worth naming honestly. Each is stated with the work that would be required to advance it.

**The 2026-05-30 hardening and the honest frame.** A 21-agent adversarial attack on this implementation produced a sharper self-assessment than the five limitations below, and it is recorded here rather than buried. The honest one-line frame: *what is deployed is an auditable energy-style salience signal — it observes and scores every control-plane transition (telemetry); it is not yet a gate that blocks them.* Three corrections followed from the attack and are now in the code and tests: (1) the **masking attack** — offsetting a protected-path violation by inflating verified evidence in the same transition — is closed by a hard, non-offsettable veto moved into the live `gateProposal()`; (2) the verified-evidence **credit was unbounded-linear**, which made the boundedness — and therefore the Lyapunov — claim vacuous; it now saturates (`−ι·log1p(min(count, cap))`), giving U a finite infimum; (3) the **KL term threw to infinity** on a provable lie and was silently skipped, accepting the worst case; it now clamps to a finite value. A second adversarial pass (2026-05-30, an automated multi-agent workflow) closed four more: a **KL length-mismatch** that skipped-to-accept (the zero-value clamp had only half-closed the drift hole); a **fractional/out-of-range outcome** that evaded the repeated-failure penalty and could flip the gate to accept — now backed by a fail-closed malformed-input term; a **symlink path-canonicalization bypass** of the operator write-guard (lexical paths let a coworker reach guard files through a symlink); and an **open-charset privacy key-guard** that still admitted lowercase secrets and was split-bypassable — now a closed-set projection over the canonical label enum, applied on both the trace and the published-export paths. A **term-count reconciliation**: §3's six-primitive presentation is the epistemic core, but the implementation evaluates ten weighted terms plus a fail-closed malformed-input guard, and the live PostToolUse path currently feeds five of them (ι verified-evidence credit, γ logLoss, δ Brier, κ repeated-failure, η protected-path); the remaining terms (α entropy, β KL, ε info-gain, ζ staleness, θ ladder-inversion) are wired in `computeU` but never populated by the live transition builder, and ζ is wired-but-dead because live evidence items carry no halfLife. A future revision should either fold the remaining terms into the formal composition or mark them implementation-only. The word "Lyapunov" is retained only in the qualified sense established in §3 (a descent-criterion-enforced, bounded-below *candidate*), never as a proven Lyapunov function.

The weights (α through κ — the six epistemic primitives plus the control-plane integrity terms and the repeated-failure penalty) are hand-tuned by the operator, not learned from transition data. The current `DEFAULT_WEIGHTS` reflects one operator's judgment about penalty severity — it is auditable and controllable, and those properties are the design's main virtue. A learned-weight variant would advance this: an adaptive-calibration loop that adjusts weights in response to operator-validated transitions, letting empirical feedback replace manual judgment over time while preserving the gate's structural form.

The strict-descent gate guarantees monotonic improvement toward a local minimum, not global convergence — established in Section 3 and carried here as continuity. A stochastic exploration variant — accepting ΔU > 0 transitions under controlled annealing conditions — would advance this toward global descent without abandoning the gate architecture.

The gate is not yet wired into the live dispatch infrastructure. `gateProposal()` is callable as a standalone function and passes its tests; it has not been bound as middleware to the agent-routing layer. A dispatcher wrapper that intercepts lane calls, evaluates `gateProposal()` pre-flight, and routes rejections back as structured signals would advance this — converting the gate from a tested library function to a runtime enforcement layer.

The gate has been adversarially attacked once (the 2026-05-30 21-agent pass) and the headline evasions are now closed and regression-tested — the violation-masking attack is vetoed, the provable-lie KL is finite, and repeated confidently-wrong forecasts no longer plateau. What remains untested is the subtler evasion class: a targeted update that minimizes one high-weight term while degrading several lower-weight terms below detection could still produce ΔU ≤ 0 while moving epistemic state in a direction that fails operator review. A standing red-team protocol — generating adversarial state pairs against the live composition, measuring evasion rate, and hardening the weakest weight components on each round — would advance this from a one-time pass to a continuous guarantee.

YURI is one operator's substrate, and the proposal has not been evaluated under concurrent state changes, shared-state contention, or multi-operator deployments where agents with different trust levels submit competing transitions. The ICM/MWP architecture is inherently single-workspace; a multi-tenant extension would require partitioned U evaluations per tenant and a global non-interference invariant at the shared-state boundary.

Section 5 names what is known to be incomplete. What is not yet known — whether the Lyapunov property holds under adversarial pressure, whether the weight composition transfers across agent architectures, and whether the gate's guarantees compose with other safety layers — is the territory Section 6 maps.
