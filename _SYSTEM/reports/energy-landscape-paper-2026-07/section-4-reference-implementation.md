# Section 4: Reference Implementation — YURI

Section 3's mechanism — a scalar potential U gated by strict descent — is not only describable; it is built. The reference implementation is YURI, an independently developed single-operator control plane created by the paper's author as a working substrate for the author's own agent-orchestration workflows. YURI is not a published platform and not a multi-tenant product. It is one operator's working environment, and it happens to instantiate the proposal of Section 3 in a form that runs, tests, and produces verifiable numeric output.

The core of the energy implementation lives in a single module: `_SYSTEM/Scripts/math/yuri-energy.mjs`. The module exports four surfaces: `computeU()`, which evaluates the scalar potential over a state snapshot; `computeDeltaU()`, which computes the signed difference between two states; `gateProposal()`, which combines both evaluations into an accept/reject decision; and `DEFAULT_WEIGHTS`, the operator-tunable weight vector. Section 3's pseudocode used underscore notation (`KL_divergence`, `log_loss`) for readability — the actual implementation uses camelCase throughout, consistent with the surrounding math kernel. The canonical import is `import { computeU, computeDeltaU, gateProposal, DEFAULT_WEIGHTS }`.

`DEFAULT_WEIGHTS` encodes the full composition — nine weighted terms, ten after the 2026-05-30 hardening (§3): protected-path violations and promotion-ladder inversions carry high penalty weight; entropy and KL divergence carry moderate positive weights; verified-evidence credit carries a negative, saturating weight that rewards epistemic progress without buying unbounded masking budget; and a repeated-failure penalty raises U per confidently-wrong forecast. These defaults are hand-tuned for a single-operator deployment, not learned from data, and not validated at production scale. The weight surface is intentionally exposed — the mechanism is designed to be operator-configured, and `DEFAULT_WEIGHTS` is the starting point rather than the final word.

The worked example that ships with the module runs two scenarios. Scenario A models a well-behaved transition: a claim that gains verified evidence support. The gate evaluates `computeDeltaU()` and returns `ΔU = -0.168578102` (post-hardening; the magnitude is smaller than the pre-saturation credit, because the evidence credit is now bounded). The component breakdown is dominated by a drop in KL divergence as claimed and verified distributions converge, with a further small descent contribution from the saturating verified-evidence credit. (Note: this shipped worked example exercises the entropy and KL terms; the live PostToolUse telemetry path feeds only the verified-evidence-credit, calibration, repeated-failure, and protected-path terms and never produces a KL contribution — see §5.) The gate accepts: the transition is a certified non-worsening step, and the dominant cause of descent is traceable.

Scenario B models a protected-path violation. An agent proposes a state change that reads a path outside its sanctioned scope. The `protectedPathViolations` term fires at full penalty weight (`ΔU = 100`), and — after the hardening — a **hard, non-offsettable veto** rejects the transition regardless of ΔU, evidence credit, override, or zeroed weight (`reason: "HARD VETO, non-offsettable"`). The rejection verdict is issued without consulting the model that generated the proposal; the gate operates on the state description alone. The violation is detectable and auditable at the control-plane layer, independent of the model. One scope honesty (carried to §5): this is the gate's evaluated *verdict* — in the current deployment the energy layer observes and scores transitions at PostToolUse and emits the signal; it does not yet intercept and block them on the live dispatch path. "Rejects" here describes the decision the gate returns, not a wired enforcement hook.

Every result envelope produced by `computeU()`, `computeDeltaU()`, and `gateProposal()` carries two fields that reflect the implementation's epistemological posture: `advisory_only: true` and `local_truth_claim: false`. The `advisory_only` flag is YURI's architectural commitment: energy evaluations are inputs to a decision, not substitutes for deterministic local evidence. A gate result of `accept: true` means the transition clears the energy condition — it does not certify that the underlying claim is factually correct, and it does not override a hard block from a higher-authority gate. That distinction is preserved by design and enforced by the flag's presence in every response envelope.

The test suites now total over 200 cases across the energy modules: unit coverage of each primitive, integration coverage of the composition rule, a CLI end-to-end that confirms both worked scenarios produce the stated output, a realistic multi-step descent trace that verifies monotonic U decrease, and — after the 2026-05-30 hardening — a dedicated adversarial-regression suite that codifies each closed attack (finite-KL-on-provable-lie, the non-offsettable protected-path veto, the dropped secret-as-key, the per-event repeated-failure penalty, and the saturating bounded-below credit). All pass. The implementation is, in that sense, complete as a tested library. It is **not yet wired as blocking middleware on the live dispatch layer** — today the energy layer runs as PostToolUse telemetry that observes and scores; calling `gateProposal()` as a pre-flight enforcement hook from the agent-routing infrastructure is the integration step that would convert observation into enforcement. At this scale that step is single-operator work, not a paper contribution, and it is named here for scope transparency.

Section 5 examines the design trade-offs this implementation leaves open: where hand-tuned weights are sufficient, where they are not, and what the honest boundary between the energy-gating guarantee and the broader goal of agent coherence actually is.
