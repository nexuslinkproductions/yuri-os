## Learnings

1. **Adversarial verification pays for itself on substrate.** Every Codex round on the energy modules surfaced a real bug a single build pass missed — most importantly a privacy-leak vector (`toJSON` hijacking `JSON.stringify`). The discipline of "no output trusted until attacked" is not ceremony; it is the cheapest place to catch the expensive failures.
2. **A validator that gates content for JSON serialization must model serialization-time behavior** — `toJSON`, getters, functions, symbols, BigInt — not just the static field shape. Pre-validate → serialize → re-validate is the canonical defense.
3. **Workflows + Opus 4.8 give genuine parallel leverage** for fan-out work (independent critique lenses, independent builds) — but synthesis of a single-voice artifact stays single-author; fanning out the writing fragments the voice.
4. **Most of a methodology paper's evidence does not require the slow real-traffic window.** Component ablation, adversarial probing, and retroactive evaluation on historical state run on synthetic/historical data — days, not weeks.
5. **Honesty is the paper's differentiator, not its weakness.** A reviewer (simulated Jan/Jake) attacks the overclaim first; a paper that pre-states exactly where the proof is and isn't removes the attack surface.
