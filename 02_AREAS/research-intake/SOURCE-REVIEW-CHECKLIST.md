# Source Review Checklist

Use this before accepting a source as reliable documentation.

---

## Identity

- Who produced the source?
- Is the author or organization identifiable?
- Is the publisher credible for this topic?
- Is the source original or a repost?

## Recency

- When was it published?
- Has the topic changed since then?
- Is the information still current?
- Does the source have a more recent revision?

## Evidence

- Does the source show direct evidence?
- Does it cite primary material?
- Are claims separated from commentary?
- Can the claim be verified elsewhere?

## Bias And Scope

- What is the source trying to achieve?
- Is the source promotional, technical, editorial, or observational?
- Does it omit important counterevidence?
- Is it narrower or broader than the claim suggests?

## Applicability

- Does the source answer the exact question?
- Is the context similar enough to matter?
- Can the claim be generalized safely?
- What assumptions would break the conclusion?

## Confidence Rating

- High: direct, current, corroborated
- Medium: useful but incomplete
- Low: weakly supported or dated

## Required Output

Every reviewed source should leave behind:
- Source name
- URL
- Access date
- Key claim
- Evidence summary
- Confidence rating
- Interpretation
- Next action
