# Archive Policy

**Purpose:** Define when research leaves active circulation and how it stays recoverable.

---

## Principle

Archive is not deletion. Archive means the material is no longer active, but remains available for traceability and future reuse.

## Archive Criteria

Move a note toward archive when:
- The question has been answered
- The conclusion is stable enough for future reference
- The note is superseded by a better synthesis
- The topic is no longer being monitored
- The work has been closed intentionally

## Do Not Archive Yet If

- The note still informs an active decision
- The source may change soon
- The evidence is incomplete
- The conclusion is still disputed
- The note is serving as a watchlist

## Archive Process

1. Mark the note as archived.
2. Add a final dated update if needed.
3. Link to the successor note or synthesis.
4. Preserve the source trail.
5. Move the note to the archive area only if the structure benefits from it.

## Naming Convention

Use explicit names so archived notes remain understandable later.

Examples:
- `TOPIC - synthesis`
- `TOPIC - decision`
- `TOPIC - archive`

## Preservation Rules

- Keep URLs if they are still meaningful.
- Keep the most important claims visible near the top.
- Do not bury the conclusion.
- Add a short note on what made the topic close.

## Recovery Rules

An archived note should still answer:
- What was being studied?
- What was concluded?
- Why did it close?
- Where is the durable version?

## Archive Boundary

If a note needs to be cited later, it should remain findable from the MOC or the research library.
