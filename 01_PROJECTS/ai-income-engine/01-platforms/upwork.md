**How work is actually obtained:** Upwork is fundamentally a job-board / proposal marketplace, NOT a gig catalog like Fiverr. Clients POST jobs ("Build me a Next.js dashboard," "Edit 12 reels with AI tools," "Write 20 SEO articles"). Freelancers BROWSE those posted jobs and APPLY by sending a proposal. This is the opposite of Fiverr's "list a gig and wait" model. Both models technically exist on Upwork (you also have a profile that clients can search and you can list "Project Catalog" productized offers), but the dominant, money-now path is applying to posted jobs.

The mechanic, step by step (2026):
1. You browse the job feed, filtered by skill/budget/client-verified status.
2. To submit a proposal you spend CONNECTS — Upwork's virtual application currency. Each Connect costs $0.15; minimum purchase 10 ($1.50). A standard job costs ~6 Connects to apply ($0.90); large/featured/high-budget jobs cost 10-16 Connects ($1.50-$2.40). The cost is dynamic and shown before you submit. [source: upworkalerts.com, snipework.com, useoutbid.com]
3. Basic (free) accounts get 10 free Connects/month; Freelancer Plus ($19.99/mo) gets 100/month plus rollover. [source: support.upwork.com, support.upwork.com/40444180130067]
4. You write a proposal (cover letter + answers to client screening questions + your rate). Uma, Upwork's built-in AI, can draft/refine proposals; Plus members get unlimited Uma, Basic gets ~10 messages/week. [source: upwork.com/resources/how-to-create-a-proposal-that-wins-jobs]
5. Optionally you BOOST the proposal: a real-time Connects auction for the top 4 "boosted" slots at the top of the client's applicant list. You set a max bid in extra Connects; the 4 highest bids win the slots; auction closes after 7 days or first hire. [source: support.upwork.com/4406541109011-Boosted-Proposals, zenlance.net]
6. Client reviews proposals, may message/interview, then sends an offer (hourly or fixed-price). You accept; work begins; for hourly you run the Upwork desktop tracker (Work Diary) for payment protection; fixed-price uses milestone escrow.
7. You get paid via the platform (ACH free, intl bank $0.99, wire $50, Instant Pay $2). [source: gigradar.io/blog/upwork-withdraw-money-fastest]

So yes: you actively apply to posted jobs. That is the core engine.

**AI policy / ban risk:** MODERATE and manageable — this is the field that kills sloppy operators, not skilled ones. Upwork explicitly PERMITS AI-assisted and AI-generated work; it is not banned. The 2026 rules center on three things [source: support.upwork.com Ethics of AI, terms.law 2025-12-05, getmany.com]:

1. DISCLOSURE: You must disclose to the client whether/how you used AI on the deliverable. Hiding it is the violation.
2. NO MISREPRESENTATION: You cannot pass off raw AI output as bespoke human work, and absolutely cannot use AI if the client's contract prohibits it. Doing so = misrepresentation, which can trigger contract termination and account action.
3. NO IP INFRINGEMENT + HUMAN OVERSIGHT: AI output must not infringe third-party copyright, and you must show human review/oversight of quality.

Separate but critical — DATA TRAINING (effective Jan 5, 2026): accounts are OPTED IN by default to let Upwork train AI on work product, messages, and platform data. Work product + communications use double opt-in (both client and freelancer must consent). For client confidentiality, go to Account Settings -> AI Preferences and OPT OUT of Work Product and Communications training on day one. [source: terms.law, support.upwork.com/23808634769683]

Ban risk that actually matters for a 2-person unit: (a) running multiple accounts deceptively — each profile must be a real, separately ID-verified human (Marcel and Mike are fine; one person faking two is a ban). (b) Off-platform contact to dodge fees = permanent suspension. (c) Undisclosed AI on a no-AI contract. Stay transparent, verify both humans, opt out of data training, and the risk is low.

# Upwork Deep-Dive — The Job-Board You Actually Apply To (2026)

## The mental model you're missing
You looked at Fiverr and saw only people *offering* gigs with no "jobs to apply for." Upwork is the opposite engine. Clients **post jobs**; freelancers **apply with proposals**. There's a profile-search layer and a "Project Catalog" (Fiverr-style productized listings) too, but the money-now path on Upwork is: read the job feed, spend Connects, send a tailored proposal, win the contract. For a high-skill operator + strong AI stack, this is the better of the two platforms because you can *go hunt demand* instead of waiting to be found. [source: upwork.com/resources/how-to-create-a-proposal-that-wins-jobs]

## How the machine works (the parts that cost you)
- **Connects** = application currency. $0.15 each, min 10. Standard job ~6 Connects ($0.90); big/featured jobs 10-16 ($1.50-$2.40). Free plan: 10/month. Plus ($19.99/mo): 100/month + rollover. [source: upworkalerts.com, snipework.com, support.upwork.com/40444180130067]
- **Proposals**: cover letter + screening answers + your bid. Uma (Upwork's built-in AI) drafts/refines them; unlimited on Plus. Don't let Uma write generic slop — clients see 50 Uma-shaped proposals a day.
- **Boosted Proposals**: a Connects auction for the top-4 slots in the applicant list. You set a max bid; 4 highest win; closes after 7 days or first hire. Early on, skip boosting and win on quality/speed/niche — you'll lose bidding wars to funded agencies. [source: support.upwork.com/4406541109011]
- **JSS (Job Success Score)**: rolling client-satisfaction metric, recalculated daily over 6/12/24-mo windows (best shown). Doesn't appear until 10+ billed hours or one completed fixed contract. Weighs long-term relationships and higher-value projects more. [source: support.upwork.com/211068358]
- **Badges**: *Rising Talent* (new accounts, needs a fully completed profile + good early behavior) bridges the no-JSS gap. *Top Rated* = JSS ≥90% for 13 of last 16 weeks + $1,000+ earned across clients in the past year, checked every 2 weeks, all criteria simultaneously. *Top Rated Plus* and *Expert-Vetted* sit above. [source: gigradar.io/blog/how-to-become-top-rated-on-upwork, support.upwork.com/211063228]
- **Hourly vs fixed**: Hourly runs the desktop tracker (Work Diary) = payment-protected, best for ambiguous/ongoing work. Fixed-price uses milestone escrow = best for scoped deliverables and faster early reviews. Start fixed-price on small scoped jobs to bank reviews fast.
- **Fees**: variable Freelancer Service Fee **0-15%** (most ~10%), shown and locked at proposal/offer time. Replaced the old tiered 20/10/5 in May 2025. [source: support.upwork.com/211062538, gigradar.io/blog/upwork-fees]

## ToS on AI — don't faceplant here
AI work is *allowed*. The rules: (1) **disclose** AI use to the client; (2) **never misrepresent** AI output as pure human work, and never use AI on a no-AI contract; (3) **no IP infringement + show human oversight**. Separately, as of Jan 5 2026 accounts are **opted IN by default** to AI training on work product/messages — go to Account Settings -> AI Preferences and **opt out** on day one to protect client confidentiality. Ban triggers that matter: fake/duplicate accounts, off-platform fee-dodging, undisclosed AI on prohibited contracts. [source: support.upwork.com Ethics of AI, terms.law 2025-12-05, support.upwork.com/23808634769683]

## Gotchas
- Connects bleed: don't apply to bloated 50+ applicant jobs or stale posts. Speed + fit beats volume.
- The cold-start tax is real — month 1 is buying reviews, not earning a salary. Plan for it.
- Don't let Uma/Claude produce obviously-AI proposals; the differentiator is *specific to their post*.
- Each profile must be a genuine separate human. One person, two accounts = ban.
- Withdrawal name must exactly match verified ID. Use ACH (free) not wire ($50).
- The variable fee means you don't know your exact cut until the offer — read it before accepting.
