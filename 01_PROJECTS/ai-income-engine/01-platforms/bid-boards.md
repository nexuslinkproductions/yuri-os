## Lower-Tier Bid Boards: Freelancer.com / PeoplePerHour / Guru / Workana — 2026 Deep Dive

### First, the confusion Marcel flagged: gig marketplace vs bid board
Fiverr confused you because it is a **gig marketplace** — you list a fixed "gig" and wait, or Fiverr's AI "Briefs" engine quietly forwards a buyer's project to a few matched sellers (Buyer Requests were killed in 2023) [source: help.fiverr.com]. You rarely just scroll open jobs and apply. **These four platforms are the opposite — true bid boards.** Clients post projects; you browse an open feed and submit a proposal/bid. That is the "apply to jobs" model you were looking for. Upwork is also a bid board, but premium-gated. These four are the lower-tier, lower-friction, lower-rate cousins.

### How each one actually works in 2026
- **Freelancer.com** — open job feed, bid with a cover note + price. Free = ~6 bids/mo + $20 min balance. Freelancer fee **10% or $5 (whichever greater)** fixed/hourly; clients pay 3%. Membership upgrades buy more bids + lower fees [source: freelancer.com/feesandcharges]. Huge volume, lowest average client quality, heaviest spam/scam exposure.
- **Guru.com** — cleanest, lowest fees of the group. Free = 10 bids/mo at **9%** job fee; paid tiers $8.95-$49.95/mo give 50 bids/mo and drop the fee to **7% / 6% / 5%** (Executive). Optional eCheck/wire gives 100% cashback on the 2.9% handling fee [source: guru.com/pricing-freelancer]. Smaller buyer pool but less brutal price war.
- **PeoplePerHour** — UK/EU-leaning, **gated**: apply for seller approval first (high rejection rate). 15 free proposal credits/mo, 1 per proposal, no free rollover [source: support.peopleperhour.com]. Fee is **tiered per client**: 20% on first £250, 7.5% to £5k, 3.5% above [source: support.peopleperhour.com]. Best economics on REPEAT clients.
- **Workana** — LATAM-first (ES/PT, strong MX/BR/AR/CO base), global-capable. Fee **20% first $300 / 10% to $3k / 5% above**, clients pay 4.5%. Local payment rails (TED, SPEI). Lower English-market competition for AI/dev work.

### What sells, and where AI specifically WINS
The market data is unambiguous and it points straight at Marcel's skillset. Upwork's 2026 report: AI-related skill demand **more than doubled (+109% YoY)**, with **AI video generation & editing +329%**, **AI integration +178%**, **AI data annotation +154%**, **AI chatbot dev +71%** [source: investors.upwork.com]. Meanwhile platforms are flooding with low-effort AI bulk sellers and clients are doing routine logo/blog/copy work themselves in ChatGPT — Fiverr/Upwork app downloads fell 18-22% as commodity tasks evaporate [source: selfemployed.com].

The takeaway: **commodity AI work is a race to the bottom; AI-augmented expert OUTCOMES are where the money moved.** A buyer can prompt Runway themselves and get garbage. They cannot get a finished, color-graded, motion-designed 60-second explainer with real edit craft — which is exactly Marcel's video/post/motion-graphics core. Same logic for working automations and shipped web apps versus "here's a prompt." **Sell the finished thing, not the AI.**

The 2-person unit's structural edge: most bidders here are solo, slow, and either pure-cheap-overseas or non-technical. A Marcel+Mike+YURI unit can (a) respond to a brief within minutes with a tailored proposal, (b) attach a real sample/proof-of-concept produced same-day that nobody else can match, and (c) deliver in hours what others quote in days. On bid boards, **speed + a concrete sample in the proposal is the single biggest win-rate lever.**

### Pricing strategy (avoid the bottom without scaring new-account buyers)
- Month 1 you have no reviews — price slightly under the credible-mid bracket, NOT rock-bottom (lowball signals "no idea what I'm doing" to good clients and only attracts bad ones). Win the first ~5-8 jobs primarily to bank 5-star reviews, treat margin as secondary.
- Lead with **fixed-price productized outcomes** ("AI explainer video, 60s, scripted + voiced + edited, 48h — $450") not hourly. Fixed price hides your speed advantage as pure margin and avoids hourly rate comparison.
- After 5+ reviews on a platform, raise 20-40% and let PPH/Guru fee tiers reward repeat clients.

### Gotchas
- **Race to the bottom is real** — Freelancer.com complaints center on lowball bids and scam-heavy feeds; Guru 3.8/Trustpilot, PPH 3.9, Freelancer 4.0 [source: bestjobsearchapps.com]. Filter ruthlessly; ignore "$30 for a full app" posts.
- **Bid scarcity = paid membership is near-mandatory** to compete on Freelancer; budget $10-$50/mo per active platform.
- **Scams**: anyone pushing WhatsApp/Skype, upfront payment from you, or off-platform work is a scam — bail [source: freelancer.com].
- **PPH gate**: don't burn your 3 rejections; build the profile first.
- **Verdict vs Upwork**: Upwork has better clients and rates but harder gating and 10-13% effective fees [source: freelancecompare.com]. These four are a faster cold-start and good for banking reviews + LATAM/UK niches, but they are a **review-farming and supplementary lane, not a primary income engine for a high-skill unit.** Recommended posture: run Guru + Freelancer + PPH as a 2-week review-building sprint, then pivot the proven samples + reviews into Upwork and direct outreach where the real rates live.
