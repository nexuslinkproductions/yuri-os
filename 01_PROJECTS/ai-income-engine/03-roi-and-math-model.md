## Part 0 — The Fiverr confusion, killed in three sentences

You keep seeing only "gigs" on Fiverr because **Fiverr is a gig marketplace, not a job board.** Sellers post a fixed productized offer ("I will build you an n8n automation for $150"), buyers come to *you* and click buy — there is **nothing to "apply" to.** [knowledge, ~2025-2026; confirmed by digest]

The platforms where you **apply to posted jobs** (a client writes "I need X, send proposals") are **Upwork, Freelancer.com, PeoplePerHour, Guru, Workana, Wellfound, and Contra's Opportunities board.** Toptal/Gun.io/Braintrust are *vetted networks* — you apply to the network once, get matched after passing a screen, then no per-job bidding.

**So the two acquisition shapes are:** *(a) build a storefront and wait to be found* (Fiverr, Gumroad), vs *(b) hunt and pitch open job posts* (Upwork et al.), vs *(c) go off-platform and create demand yourself* (cold outreach, Reddit, productized landing page). Your unit should run **(b) + (c) as the engine and (a) as a passive funnel** — reasoning below.

**Review-velocity starvation.** Everything compounds off the first 3–5 reviews per profile. If W1–2 produces zero banked reviews (proposals ignored, a client ghosts, a delivery slips, or — worst case — **an automated Fiverr/Upwork ban for perceived raw-AI resale** [digest: Fiverr 2026 enforcement wave is automated, bans within hours, earnings frozen ≤180 days]), the entire month collapses to the conservative floor or below, because **you never escape the no-trust equilibrium.**

## Part 2 — Instrumenting the sprint as a live example for the mathematics paper

You want the real-money sprint to *also* be a worked dynamical-systems example. Good instinct — it's a genuine controlled stochastic process. Here's the honest map of **where the math is load-bearing vs. where it's decoration.** I'll flag both, because dressing a spreadsheet in Lyapunov notation and calling it a theorem is exactly the kind of thing your Codex final-pass would (correctly) reject.

### 2.1 State-space formulation (this part is real)

Model the unit as a discrete-time controlled dynamical system, day or week index $t$. Define the **state vector**:

$$
x_t = \begin{bmatrix} B_t \\ R_t \\ r_t \\ M_t \end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}\text{backlog (committed work, in AI-adjusted hours)}\\ \text{reputation capital (review count / JSS proxy)}\\ \text{revenue rate (\$ / week, smoothed)}\\ \text{recurring MRR (\$ / month)}\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Control inputs** $u_t = \begin{bmatrix} e_t \\ a_t \\ p_t \\ \pi_t \end{bmatrix}$ = (operator effort allocation, AI-leverage allocation, proposal/outreach volume, price level).

**Why this is honest:** backlog, review count, revenue rate, and MRR are all genuinely *observable, measurable daily states*, and effort/AI-allocation/proposals/price are genuinely *choosable controls*. The system genuinely has memory (today's reviews gate tomorrow's win rate). This is a real state-space model, not a metaphor.

### 2.2 The dynamics (where it's part-real, part-stylized)

A defensible nonlinear update:

$$
\begin{aligned}
B_{t+1} &= B_t + \underbrace{w(R_t)\,p_t}_{\text{new wins inflow}} - \underbrace{\frac{e_t \cdot \ell(a_t)}{h}}_{\text{throughput out}} \\[4pt]
R_{t+1} &= R_t + \gamma \cdot \mathbb{1}[\text{delivery completed} \wedge \text{5★}] - \delta\,\mathbb{1}[\text{ban/dispute}] \\[4pt]
r_{t+1} &= \beta\, r_t + (1-\beta)\big(\text{completed jobs}\times \bar{V}\big) \\[4pt]
M_{t+1} &= M_t + (\text{retainers closed}) - (\text{churn})
\end{aligned}
$$

where $w(R_t)$ = **win-rate as an increasing saturating function of reputation** (e.g. $w(R)=w_\infty\frac{R}{R+R_{1/2}}$), $\ell(a_t)$ = AI-leverage multiplier on throughput, $h$ = hours/job, $\bar V$ = average ticket, $\beta$ = revenue-smoothing/inertia.

- **Real / defensible:** the *structure* — win rate saturating in reputation ($w(R)$ monotone, bounded), backlog as an inflow–outflow accumulator (a literal queue), AI acting **multiplicatively on the service rate but not on the arrival/trust rate** (this is the precise formalization of "AI helps you deliver, not get trusted"). These are mechanisms you can actually defend.
- **Stylized / would need fitting:** the *functional forms and parameters* ($\gamma, \delta, R_{1/2}, w_\infty, \beta$). You have **n=1 unit and 4 weeks of data** — you can *illustrate* the model and *estimate* parameters loosely, you cannot *validate* it. Say so in the paper.

### 2.3 Backlog as a queue — this is the strongest, most legitimate piece

Drop the reputation layer for a moment and look at backlog $B_t$ alone. It's a textbook **queueing/inventory system**: work arrives at rate $\lambda_t = w(R_t)p_t$ (wins), is served at rate $\mu_t = e_t \ell(a_t)/h$ (AI-leveraged throughput). Classic stability condition:

$$
\boxed{\;\rho_t = \frac{\lambda_t}{\mu_t} < 1\;}
$$

If arrival (wins) persistently exceeds service capacity, backlog diverges → late deliveries → bad reviews → the reputation state $R_t$ *collapses* → the whole system destabilizes. **This is a real, decision-useful result, not decoration:** it says *don't win work faster than your AI-leveraged unit can deliver at 5★ quality.* The coupling $B_t \to$ (delivery quality) $\to R_t \to \lambda_t$ is precisely why over-winning is self-destructive — a genuine feedback instability, and it's the formal version of the §1.5 risk.

### 2.4 Lyapunov / stability analysis — honest scope

Here's where I separate the load-bearing claim from the decorative one.

**What "stability" should mean for *this* system:** not "revenue stops changing" (you *want* revenue to grow). The right notion is **stability of the income *process* around a sustainable, self-renewing operating point** — i.e., the system reaches a regime where revenue rate is maintained or grows *without continued external capital injection or unsustainable overwork*, and recovers from shocks (a churned client, a slow week). Formally: **convergence to a positively-invariant operating set, and robustness of that set to bounded disturbances.**

**Candidate Lyapunov function (for the backlog/quality subsystem — where it's legitimate):**

$$
V(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}\big(B - B^\star\big)^2 \;+\; \tfrac{\kappa}{2}\big(r^\star - r\big)^2
$$

where $B^\star$ is a target *non-zero* backlog (you want a healthy pipeline, not an empty one) and $r^\star$ is the target sustainable revenue rate. $V \ge 0$, $V=0$ only at the target. **The control objective** is to choose $u_t$ (effort, AI-leverage, proposal volume, price) so that $\Delta V_t = V(x_{t+1}) - V(x_t) \le 0$ along trajectories — i.e., each week you act to *reduce the gap* to the sustainable operating point. A control law that does this — *throttle proposal volume $p_t$ up when backlog is below $B^\star$, down when above; raise AI-leverage $a_t$ when backlog spikes; raise price $\pi_t$ as $R_t$ grows* — is a genuine **feedback controller**, and "the sprint plan is a hand-tuned version of this controller" is a *true and useful* statement. Weeks 1→4 literally implement: build pipeline (raise $B$ toward $B^\star$), then stabilize quality, then raise price as $R$ accumulates.

**Where the analogy holds (claim these):**
1. **Queue stability $\rho<1$** — fully legitimate, decision-useful, provable for the backlog subsystem.
2. **Reputation as a slow integrator gating a fast revenue loop** — real two-timescale structure; the cold-start trap *is* an unstable equilibrium at $R=0$ that you must be kicked out of by an external input (loss-leader orders / outside traffic). That "you need an external impulse to escape the $R\approx0$ basin" is a genuine and elegant dynamical reading of why month 1 needs deliberate underpricing.
3. **Lyapunov-guided feedback control of backlog/quality** — defensible as a *control-design illustration*, with hand-tuned (not optimized) gains.

**Where it's decorative (label these honestly in the paper):**
1. **Any global convergence/optimality "theorem."** With n=1 and unfitted nonlinear $w,\ell$, you can simulate and illustrate, not prove convergence of the real business.
2. **Treating reputation growth as smooth.** Reviews are discrete, sparse, and partly exogenous (client whim, ToS bans = a jump/impulsive term $-\delta$). A ban is a **discontinuous catastrophic reset** of $R$, not a smooth perturbation — Lyapunov *decrease* says nothing about surviving a state jump that large. This is the §1.5 risk in formal clothing, and it's the cleanest example of where the smooth-system math *fails to capture the real risk* — worth stating explicitly as a limitation, it's more honest and more interesting than pretending it's covered.
3. **"Stable" ≠ "successful."** A unit stuck at the conservative floor is *technically stable* (low fixed point). The math describes dynamics around an operating point; it does **not** certify that operating point is good. Don't let stability language smuggle in a success claim.

### 2.5 What to actually log during the sprint (so the paper has real data)

Daily, machine-parseable — this doubles as your business dashboard *and* your dataset:

```
DAY=t  B=<backlog_hrs>  R_marcel=<reviews>  R_mike=<reviews>
       proposals_sent=<p>  wins=<n>  win_rate=<wins/proposals_window>
       revenue_today=<$>  r_smoothed=<$/wk>  MRR=<$>
       effort_h_marcel=<e1>  effort_h_mike=<e2>  ai_leverage_est=<x>
       events=[ban|dispute|5star|retainer_closed|churn]
```

With 28 daily vectors you can: (a) estimate $w(R)$ from win-rate-vs-reputation scatter, (b) check $\rho_t<1$ empirically each week, (c) plot $V(x_t)$ and show whether the hand-tuned controller actually drove $\Delta V \le 0$, (d) mark the impulsive events. **That's a legitimate "applied dynamical systems on a real controlled process" case study** — far stronger than a toy simulation, *provided you state the n=1, short-horizon, unfitted-parameter, jump-discontinuity caveats up front.*
