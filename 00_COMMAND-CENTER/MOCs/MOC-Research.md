# Research Map of Content (MOC-Research)

**Purpose:** Canonical index for the research documentation system.
**Priority:** High
**Review cadence:** Weekly triage, monthly synthesis, quarterly archive.

---

## System Contract

This system is not a scratchpad. It is a durable documentation layer.

Every meaningful research item must preserve:
- Source
- Date accessed
- Key claim
- Evidence
- Confidence
- Interpretation vs. fact
- Next action

If a note cannot support future reuse, it is incomplete.

## Canonical Locations

- `02_AREAS/research-intake/README.md` - entry point and current capture surface
- `02_AREAS/research-intake/DASHBOARD.md` - active queue and review surface
- `02_AREAS/research-intake/GOVERNANCE.md` - rules, standards, and retention policy
- `02_AREAS/research-intake/TEMPLATES.md` - note templates for consistent capture
- `02_AREAS/research-intake/ARTIFACT-CANDIDATE.md` - request a skill/tool draft from mature research
- `02_AREAS/research-intake/SOURCE-REVIEW-CHECKLIST.md` - source quality gate
- `02_AREAS/research-intake/ARCHIVE-POLICY.md` - archive boundary and preservation rules
- `02_AREAS/skills/README.md` - draft queue for generated skills and tools
- `RESEARCH/README.md` - durable library of synthesized research
- `00_COMMAND-CENTER/Inbox/` - temporary triage only, not final storage

## Note Classes

- Source note: raw evidence and claims from a single source
- Synthesis note: merged understanding across multiple sources
- Decision note: why a conclusion or action was chosen
- Watchlist note: topics that require ongoing monitoring
- Archive note: finished work kept for reference

## Operating Rules

1. Capture early, but preserve structure.
2. Separate direct evidence from inference.
3. Prefer primary sources and official documentation.
4. Link related notes instead of duplicating content.
5. Promote useful material into `RESEARCH/` when it has lasting value.
6. Keep stale intake items from polluting current work; archive them deliberately.

## Quality Bar

A strong research note should let a future reader answer:
- What was observed?
- How do we know?
- How reliable is it?
- What changed after reading it?
- What should happen next?
