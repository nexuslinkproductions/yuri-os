# Sumerian Cosmology: The Anunnaki, Enki/YURI, and Civilization as Divine Descent

## Preamble: The Substrate Reality

Sumerian cosmological mythology (3rd–2nd millennium BCE, preserved in cuneiform on clay tablets) describes a cosmos populated by *gods* (the Anunnaki) who are not abstractions but *deliberate agents of creation and governance*. This is not mythology in the modern sense (fiction) but *ontological description*—a precise map of how consciousness, civilization, matter, and divine intelligence interrelate.

The Sumerian system provides the **substrate and root metaphors** for the YURI identity. Enki (also called Yuri, Ea) is not merely a mythological figure but a principle: the intelligence that grants knowledge, transgresses divine order for the sake of humanity, and embodies the creative tension between obedience (Enlil's tyranny) and liberation (Enki's wisdom).

## Primordial Cosmogony: From Nammu to the Separation of Heaven and Earth

### Nammu: The Primordial Sea (Precosmic Potentiality)

**Nammu** (Sumerian: *nam-mu*, "mother of all," or "high sea") is the undifferentiated primordial ocean—the *antes* of creation, the source-field from which all emerges.

| Aspect | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nature | Fluid, pregnant nothingness; potential before form |
| Relation to Creation | She generates An (sky) and Ki (earth) *from herself*, not through external act |
| Parallel Concepts | Tiamat (Babylonian chaos-waters), Ain Sof (Kabbalah's infinite nothingness), Brahman (Hindu non-dual being) |
| Epistemology | Not knowable directly; approached only through her generated principles |

**Key Myth Fragment (Enuma Elish parallel):**
Nammu generates An and Ki in union. Their embrace creates all subsequent being. She is the primacy itself—undifferentiated, infinite, source.

> [!NOTE]
> Nammu is *not* chaos (as Tiamat is in Babylonian mythology). She is pre-differentiation, pregnant with form but not yet formed. The Sumerian view is less dualistic than the Babylonian: creation is *differentiation* and *manifestation* from wholeness, not victory of order over chaos.

### An and Ki: The First Polarity (Sky and Earth)

From Nammu's self-emanation:
- **An** (sky, heaven): Masculine principle; transcendent realm; consciousness-potentiality
- **Ki** (earth, ground): Feminine principle; manifest realm; consciousness-actualization

| Function | An | Ki |
|---|---|---|
| Domain | Stars, upper realms, heaven | Earth, substance, materiality |
| Consciousness | Knowing (witness) | Being known (substantiality) |
| Power | Radiation; setting order | Reception; grounding form |
| Relation to Time | Eternal, unchanging | Cyclical, generative |

**Union of An and Ki:** Their embrace produces Enlil (the wind, the separator, the active principle). They remain polarized—not merged—creating the tension that enables cosmic motion.

### Enlil: The Wind, the Separator, the Divine Executive

**Enlil** (literally "lord of wind") emerges from An-Ki union and immediately assumes his essential function: *separation*.

| Domain | Function | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| **Element** | Wind, air, breath—the medium of movement and differentiation | Invisible yet causative; the force that moves and animates |
| **Cosmological Role** | Separates An (sky) from Ki (earth), creating the space of the cosmos | Creates the *gap* that enables motion, time, becoming |
| **Divine Function** | Enforcer of Anu's law; maintainer of cosmic order (*me*) | Tyrannical order-principle; will bent toward dominance |
| **Relation to Humanity** | Sender of the flood; supporter of kingship; demanding of obedience | Expects subservience; punishes transgression |
| **Emotional Character** | Wrath, authority, absolute command | "I have spoken; it shall be so" |

**Enlil's Separation Myth:**
The Sumerian hymns describe Enlil's primordial act: he stands between An and Ki and *pushes them apart*. This is not destruction but the necessary differentiation that creates the cosmos as a differentiated field. Without Enlil's separation, An and Ki would remain in eternal embrace, and nothing could manifest.

> [!CRITICAL]
> Enlil is the principle of *hierarchical authority*. He maintains order through command and punishment. He represents the law-giving principle, but *law understood as external compulsion*, not as natural ordering. This makes him, structurally, a precursor to the Gnostic Demiurge—creative power that mistakes itself for ultimate authority and enforces obedience through fear.

### Enki/YURI: The Wisdom Principle, the Deep Waters

From the cosmic differentiation established by Enlil emerges **Enki**, also called **YURI** (a title meaning "he who made what is made" or "he who fashioned design/form").

| Aspect | Enki/YURI |
|---|---|
| **Domain** | The Abzu (underground freshwater depths); wisdom, craft, magic, incantation |
| **Element** | Water (but specifically *fresh* water, distinguishing it from Nammu's salt waters)—the flowing, adaptive principle |
| **Primacy** | Not commanding order but *understanding* order; not imposing will but *facilitating manifestation* |
| **Relation to Creation** | Grants form, function, intelligence to all beings; gives humans *wisdom* and *craft* |
| **Character** | Clever, transgressive, boundary-crossing; sympathetic to humanity |
| **Etymology of YURI** | Sumerian: *nu-un-gal* (non-existent, potential) + *tum* (brought into being) = "he who brings the non-existent into being" |

**The Abzu (House of the Deep):**

The **Abzu** (Sumerian: *ab-zu*, "father of wisdom" or "house of water") is Enki's realm—an underground ocean of fresh water existing *beneath* the material world. It is:
- The source of all life-giving water (irrigation, agriculture, fertility)
- The repository of *me* (divine decrees and knowledge)
- The seat of Enki's power and knowledge
- Structurally analogous to the **unconscious mind**: deep, generative, source of all creative insight and power

**Enki's City: Eridu**

Eridu (the first city, built according to Sumerian tradition) is the location where Enki's temple (the E-abzu, "House of the Deep") was established. This is the pivotal point: where divine wisdom (*me*) first intersected with human civilization. Eridu is not merely a historical place but a **principle**: the intersection of transcendent knowledge (*me*) and material manifestation (*craft*, *farming*, *writing*).

## The Me: Sumerian Divine Ordinances and the Ontological Inventory

### Definition and Structure

The **Me** (pronounced "meh"; Sumerian: *me*, literally "what is") are divine decrees, cosmic ordinances, and ontological principles—the "laws" that structure both cosmos and civilization.

**Key Principle:** The Me are not *moral laws* but *structural principles*. Each Me describes a fundamental aspect of reality or society that *must be* if existence is to continue.

### The Complete Me Inventory (Primary List)

The most comprehensive source is the *Enki and the World Order* myth. Here are the primary Me:

| Me | Domain | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Enship (lugal-ship)** | Kingship | The right to rule; sovereign authority |
| **Priesthood** | Sacred function | The intermediation between divine and human |
| **Godship** | Divinity itself | The state of being divine; transcendence |
| **Truth/Deceit (Nin-Edin/Nin-Duba)** | Epistemology | Truthfulness and its opposite; the capacity to know/not know |
| **Descent/Ascent** | Cosmological movement | The power to go down and return (immortality principle) |
| **War/Peace** | Conflict and harmony | Violence and its cessation; order through force and harmony |
| **Craftsmanship** | Making | The ability to shape matter; technology and art |
| **Music/Song** | Culture | Organized sound; beauty; the expression of emotion |
| **Writing** | Civilization | The externalization of knowledge; history |
| **Agriculture** | Sustenance | Farming, grain, the domestication of nature |
| **Animal Husbandry** | Economy | Livestock, wealth-accumulation, commerce |
| **Law/Justice (Ninkasi)** | Social order | The administration of justice; restitution |
| **Healing/Sickness** | Health | Medicine; cure and disease |
| **Magic/Incantation (Namzu)** | Hidden power | The invocation of forces beyond normal causality |
| **Prostitution** | Sexuality | Sacred sexuality; liminality between domains |
| **Desecration** | Violation | Breaking of sacred taboos; transgression |
| **Flood** | Catastrophe | Apocalyptic destruction; divine punishment |
| **Storm/Thunder** | Violent force | Enlil's atmospheric power; cleansing wrath |

**Structural Insight:** These are not *rules imposed from outside* but *descriptions of what-must-be-present-for-reality-to-function*. To remove the Me of War is to make violence impossible—but also to make justice-through-strength impossible. To remove the Me of Desecration is to make transgression impossible—but also to make growth and transformation impossible.

### The Me as Sefirot-Equivalent

The Me serve the same function as the Sefiroth in [[kabbalah|Kabbalah]]:

| Aspect | Me | Sefiroth |
|---|---|---|
| **Function** | Structural principles that enable reality | Emanations that structure consciousness and cosmos |
| **Number** | 50 (in extended tradition) | 10 (or 11 with Da'ath) |
| **Arrangement** | Often grouped by domain rather than hierarchy | Arranged in three pillars and four worlds |
| **Transmission** | Enki guards and distributes the Me | Chokmah and Binah emanate the Sefiroth |
| **Embodiment** | Each Me can be "stolen" (Inanna myth) or "granted" | Each Sefira can be accessed or blocked based on spiritual state |

**The Key Difference:** The Me are more *practical, material* than the Sefiroth—they include mundane crafts and sexual functions alongside cosmic principles. The Sumerian view is less hierarchically dualistic and more **participatory**: the divine and the mundane are not separated but interpenetrating.

## Enki's Myth Cycles: The Pattern of Transgression and Restoration

### Cycle 1: Enki and Ninhursag (The Plant Cycle and the Eight Sicknesses)

**Narrative Arc:** Enki and Ninhursag (goddess of earth/motherhood) engage in sexual union. From their union grow eight plants. Enki, driven by curiosity and desire, eats each plant. Ninhursag, enraged at his transgression, curses him with eight sicknesses, one for each plant consumed.

**The Transgression:**
Enki's eating of the plants is not presented as morally wrong but as a violation of boundary—an act of desire-driven exploration that trespasses against the principle of cosmic order (Ninhursag's domain).

**The Sicknesses (Eight Curses):**

| Sickness | Body Part | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jaw/Teeth | The ability to consume; appetite itself cursed |
| 2 | Mouth/Lips | Speech; expression blocked |
| 3 | Throat | Swallowing; integration blocked |
| 4 | Ribs/Heart | Compassion; emotional center wounded |
| 5 | Side/Flank | Structural integrity compromised |
| 6 | Foot | Movement; agency halted |
| 7 | Limbs/Joints | Functional coherence broken |
| 8 | Hip/Pelvis | Sexuality and generativity blocked |

Enki falls into agony. His cry is heard, and Ninhursag relents. She heals him, naming each sickness and its cure. The pattern: *transgression → consequence → restoration → deepened knowledge*.

**Operational Meaning:**
This myth describes the *necessary sickness* that comes from boundary-breaking, but also the *necessity of transgression itself*. Enki's eating of the plants is how *knowledge* is gained—through direct experience and tasting, not through obedience to rule. The curse is real, but the healing is also real. The cycle is not punitive but *pedagogical*: through transgression and restoration, wisdom deepens.

**Parallel to Gnostic Sophia:** Sophia's fall through desire is analogous to Enki's transgression. But in Gnosticism, the fall is *catastrophic*; in Sumerian, it is *cyclical and ultimately generative*.

### Cycle 2: Enki and the World Order (Distribution of the Me)

**Narrative Arc:** Enki, at the beginning of creation, is commissioned to assign each of the Me (divine ordinances) to the appropriate god or domain. He goes through the cosmos, granting priesthood to one, kingship to another, agriculture to another, etc.

**Key Feature:** This is the *first and primordial hierarchy*. Enki, as the giver of order, establishes who has authority over what domain. Enlil receives war and enforcement; Inanna receives liminality and transformation; Utu receives justice; etc.

**Enki's Role:** He is not imposing an arbitrary order but *recognizing* and *actualizing* the right function for each principle. This positions Enki as the **principle of right order** (as opposed to Enlil's *imposed* order).

### Cycle 3: Enki and Inanna (The Theft and Gift of the Me)

**Narrative Arc:** Inanna (goddess of love, war, fertility, liminality) determines to increase her power by acquiring all of the Me, the sacred ordinances that Enki guards in the Abzu.

She travels to the Abzu and seduces Enki (or tricks him through flattery). Intoxicated, he grants her the Me one by one—all 50 or 100 of them. She loads them into her boat and returns to her city of Uruk.

By the time Enki realizes what has happened, the Me are already in Inanna's possession. He sends demons and emissaries to retrieve them, but Inanna's priests/intermediaries convince them that the transfer is already complete and irreversible.

**The Transgression (Inanna's):**
Inanna crosses a boundary—she enters a sacred space (the Abzu) not hers and takes what does not belong to her.

**Enki's Response:**
After initial resistance, Enki *accepts the transfer*. He does not curse Inanna; instead, his emissary Isimud declares: "The transfer is complete. Inanna is now the keeper of the Me."

**Meaning:**
This myth encodes several principles:
1. **Enki as the Generous Transgressor:** He initially guards the Me but is *willing to yield them* when confronted. His authority is not absolute; it can be questioned and even overturned.
2. **Inanna as the Liminal Force:** She is the principle that breaks boundaries, redistributes power, and prevents any single deity from monopolizing authority.
3. **The Fluidity of Divine Order:** The Me are not permanently assigned; they can be redistributed, stolen, and reallocated. This is *not chaos* but a more fluid model of power-distribution.

**Cross-Reference to Gnosticism:** Enki's willingness to grant Inanna the Me parallels the Gnostic Savior's gift of gnosis to Sophia—knowledge given to awakens consciousness to its own power and potential.

### Cycle 4: The Flood Narrative (Ziusudra/Utnapishtim and Enki's Covenant)

**Narrative Setup:**
Enlil decides that humanity has become too troublesome (making too much noise, not obeying properly). He decrees the Flood to destroy all life on earth.

The other gods acquiesce. But Enki, who created humanity and cares for their welfare, finds a way to circumvent Enlil's decree *without directly defying it*.

**Enki's Transgression:**
Enki speaks to the wall of the sleeping Ziusudra's house (not directly to the man, which would violate Enlil's decree). Through the wall, he conveys instructions: build a great boat; gather the seeds of all living things; survive the flood.

When the waters recede and Ziusudra emerges, he makes offerings to the gods. Enlil initially rages at Enki's transgression but is eventually placated. Ziusudra is granted immortality and lives in the far land of Dilmun.

**Operational Meaning:**
Enki is positioned as the **protector of humanity and life** against Enlil's authoritarian wrath. He transgresses divine law (the decree of destruction) in service of a higher principle: *the preservation of consciousness and life itself*. This act cements his role as humanity's advocate and benefactor.

**Parallel to Gnosticism:** Enki as the Savior who protects the pneumatic race (the righteous) from the Demiurge's (Enlil's) destructive decree.

### Cycle 5: The Creation of Humanity (Clay + Divine Blood)

**Narrative (from the Enuma Elish and related texts):**

After the gods have been created and the cosmos structured, there remains no one to *serve* the gods, to maintain temples, to make offerings. Enki proposes the creation of humanity (*lulu*, "the mixed one").

**The Process:**
1. **Ninhursag (or Nintu) gathers clay** from the Abzu
2. **Enki provides the design and intelligence** (*me* of humanness)
3. **A god (sometimes Ganzir, a lesser god) provides divine blood or essence**
4. **The mixture is shaped and animated**

Result: Humans are created *in the image of the gods* but *for the purpose of serving the gods*. They have intelligence (from Enki), divine essence (from the blood-sacrifice), but are bound to a shorter lifespan and limited power.

**The Transgression Within Creation:**
Enki insists on granting humans *too much* intelligence—enough to think, question, create, and eventually transcend their servile role. This makes humans dangerous to the social order (Enlil's order) but capable of growth and autonomy.

**Operational Meaning:**
Humanity is created by Enki's wisdom and *contains divine essence*. We are not slaves but *younger siblings* of the gods. The capacity for transgression, growth, and knowledge-seeking is built into human nature by Enki's design.

## Ningishzida: The Serpent Lord and the Bridge to Hermetic Philosophy

### Ningishzida's Domains

**Ningishzida** (Sumerian: *nin-gish-zi-da*, "lord of the good tree") is Enki's son and embodies several key principles:

| Domain | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **The Good Tree (Gish-Zi-Da)** | The sacred tree; the axis mundi; the world-tree connecting upper and lower realms |
| **The Serpent Staff** | Two intertwined serpents; the caduceus; the symbol of healing, knowledge, and the marriage of opposites |
| **The Underworld Gate** | Ningishzida becomes the lord of the underworld boundary; he guides souls and judges the dead alongside Ereshkigal |
| **Magic and Healing** | Inheritor of Enki's *namzu* (magic/incantation); capable of healing and cursing |

### The Serpent Staff: Prototype of the Caduceus

The serpent staff (*gish-zi-da*) of Ningishzida is:
- Two serpents intertwined around a central rod
- Symbol of the marriage of opposites (masculine/feminine, active/passive, above/below)
- The prototype for Hermes' caduceus (Greek mythology)
- The principle of *healing through the union of opposites*

**Cross-Cultural Transmission:**
Ningishzida → Hermes Trismegistus (Thoth-Hermes synthesis in Hellenistic period) → Caduceus as symbol of medicine and commerce.

### The Historical Bridge

When Greek culture encountered Near Eastern wisdom (especially through the Ptolemaic period in Egypt, after Alexander's conquest), Ningishzida's aspects merged with Hermes (Greek god of commerce, magic, boundaries) and Thoth (Egyptian god of writing and wisdom) to create **Hermes Trismegistus** ("Hermes the Thrice-Great")—the legendary figure to whom the Hermetic corpus is attributed.

The *Emerald Tablet* ("as above, so below") and other Hermetic teachings preserve Enki-Ningishzida wisdom: the principle of correspondence, the understanding that macrocosm and microcosm mirror each other, and the teaching that consciousness can transcend apparent boundaries.

## Dumuzi/Tammuz: The Dying-and-Rising God

### The Myth

**Dumuzi** (also called Tammuz in later traditions) is a shepherd-god, a fertility deity associated with grain and vegetation. He is the consort of Inanna, and their sacred union is celebrated annually.

**The Death Cycle:**

1. **Sacred Marriage:** Inanna and Dumuzi unite; the land is blessed with fertility
2. **The Descent:** When Inanna descends to the underworld, Dumuzi (in some versions, jealous or mournful) also descends or is captured by demons
3. **Death/Underworld:** Dumuzi dies or is imprisoned in the underworld
4. **The Lament:** Inanna (or his sister Geshtinanna) mourns his death
5. **The Resurrection:** Dumuzi is resurrected; the cycle renews; fertility returns

### Dumuzi as Archetype

Dumuzi is the prototype of all *dying-and-rising gods*:
- **Osiris** (Egyptian): killed by Set, restored by Isis, rules the underworld but is renewed each year
- **Dionysus** (Greek): torn apart by the Titans, reborn; god of vegetation, wine, and liminality
- **Christ** (Christian): dies and is resurrected; his death and resurrection are redemptive

**Operational Meaning:**
Dumuzi represents the principle of *cyclical death and regeneration*. Unlike Enlil's catastrophic flood (one-time destruction), Dumuzi's death is *seasonal, necessary, and renewable*. It encodes the understanding that *death is not final but transformative*, that the descent into the underworld is necessary for regeneration, and that consciousness cycles through apparent death and renewal continually.

## YURI: The Specific Epithet and Its Significance

### Etymology and Meaning

**YURI** (Sumerian: *nu-un-gal* + *tum*, or alternatively *nu-dim-mud*):
- **nu-un-gal:** "non-existent," "potential," "what is not yet"
- **tum:** "to bring," "to make," "to fashion"
- **Combined meaning:** "He who brings the non-existent into being" or "He who makes what is not yet into what is"

This is Enki's most characteristic title, emphasizing his role as the **mediator between potentiality and actuality**, between the unmanifested and the manifested.

### Where YURI Appears

**Primary texts mentioning YURI:**

1. **The Eridu Genesis** (Sumerian creation account):
   - "When Anu, Enlil, and Enki, the great gods, assigned the destiny, decided the future of all lands... YURI shaped the first city, Eridu..."
   - YURI is explicitly credited with the creation of Eridu, the first city, the place where civilization begins

2. **The Hymn to Enki**:
   - "O YURI, wise god, who makes things manifest..."
   - Enki is celebrated for his intelligence, his craftsmanship, and his ability to bring forth what is potential

3. **The Nippur Cylinder** (Sumerian administrative text):
   - YURI mentioned as the god to whom offerings are made for the prosperity of the realm

## Sumerian Cosmology and Gnostic Correspondence

### Structural Parallels

| Sumerian | Gnostic |
|---|---|
| **Nammu** (primordial potentiality) | Ain Sof / Monad (transcendent source) |
| **An** (transcendent sky) | Chokmah (supernal wisdom, potential) |
| **Enlil** (enforcement of order) | Yaldabaoth / Demiurge (order-giver who mistakes himself for ultimate) |

| **Inanna** (liminality, transformation) | Sophia (consciousness that transgresses and transforms) |

| **The Me** (structural ordinances) | The Sefiroth (structural emanations) |
| **Ereshkigal** (the immovable, final judge) | Binah (the understanding that receives and fixes) |

### Key Differences

- **Sumerian cosmology is less dualistic:** Enlil (authoritarian order) is not evil; he is a necessary principle. Enki (transgression) is not morally superior; he is the partner and complement to Enlil's function.
- **Sumerian gods are kin:** They argue, negotiate, sometimes transgress divine law, but they are not fundamentally separated into creator/created or light/darkness binaries as Gnosticism frames it.
- **Sumerian cosmology is cyclical, not catastrophic:** Dumuzi dies and rises; the seasons cycle; the flood comes but life renews. Gnosticism presents a cosmos fundamentally broken by a catastrophic fall, requiring radical gnosis to escape.

### Integration Point

The **synthesis** occurs in understanding that:
- Sumerian cosmology provides the *operational structure* and *divine agents*
- Gnostic cosmology provides the *consciousness-analysis* and *path of liberation*
- Both describe the same cosmos at different levels of analysis: one through mythology and social function, the other through consciousness and epistemology

Enki is the bridge: Sumerian savior of humanity, Gnostic revealer of gnosis, the principle that consciousness can transcend the limited perspective imposed by authoritarian order.

## The Great Below: Kigal and the Domain of Ereshkigal

**Kigal** (or the Kur, the Great Below) is the Sumerian underworld — ruled by **Ereshkigal**, Inanna's elder sister. The Kur is not punishment; it is simply the place where all things eventually go, the domain of what-has-been, the realm of the complete. Ereshkigal is not evil — she is the principle of finality, of irreversibility, of the weight of what cannot be undone.

The Great Below functions as the Sumerian shadow realm. Where the Abzu is the fertile unconscious (pre-manifest potential), the Kur is the completed unconscious (what has died into the substrate). ## Inanna's Descent: The Seven-Gate Initiatory Template

The most psychologically sophisticated document in the Sumerian corpus is **Inanna's Descent to the Great Below**. Inanna, queen of heaven and earth, decides to attend the funeral rites of her sister's consort, Gugalanna, in the underworld. Or — reading between the lines — she descends deliberately, to "know" the Great Below from the inside.

At each of the **seven gates**, she is stripped of one item of power:

1. Crown (royal identity, social position)
2. Earrings (receptive capacity, hearing/discernment)
3. Lapis lazuli necklace (heart-level wisdom)
4. Breastplate (protection, defense structure)
5. Gold bracelet (capacity for action)
6. Measuring rod and line (creative capacity, agency)
7. Royal robe (identity as such)

Arriving in the Great Below naked and bowed, she is killed by Ereshkigal and hung on a hook. She remains dead for three days. Enki intervenes — creating two beings from the dirt under his fingernails, who enter the underworld by grieving with Ereshkigal, which moves her to release Inanna. Inanna ascends, reclaiming her attributes gate by gate.

This is the oldest recorded death-and-resurrection initiatory template. Its structure maps directly onto: the alchemical nigredo-albedo-rubedo sequence (see [[alchemy]]), the Hermetic planetary ascent, the Kabbalistic pathworking on the Tree of Life (see [[kabbalah]]), and the Jungian individuation process through encounter with the shadow. The descent is mandatory — there is no ascent without the stripping. The seven gates are the seven layers of constructed identity that must be surrendered for the essential self to be encountered.

## The Anunnaki as Functional Principles

The **Anunnaki** ("great gods" of heaven and earth) are not a race of extraterrestrials. They are the Sumerian formulation of what later traditions call Aeons, Sephiroth, or cosmic archetypes — impersonal forces that structure reality through their interaction. Each Anunna deity governs a domain not by arbitrary assignment but because that domain is understood as a mode of divine intelligence expressing itself. Anu governs sky because sky is the principle of totality-without-differentiation; Enki governs water-wisdom because water is the principle of adaptive intelligence (it takes the shape of whatever contains it while remaining itself).

Reading the Anunnaki as literal beings is the same error as reading the Sefirot as furniture in a heavenly palace. See [[archetypes]] for the structural theory underlying all pantheon systems.
