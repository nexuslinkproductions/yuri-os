The signal problem, visualized

Layer A — 32 price factors (1-minute)

These already exist and drive every trade. The instinct "we need a 1-minute factor" is already satisfied — 32 of them. The problem is they are all algebraic shadows of the same 1m price series, so they fire together and agree together.

Effective number of independent bets (eff-N) ≈ 1.0–1.7. That is "one bet wearing 32 costumes," not 32 independent edges. At 99% of timestamps exactly one factor fires.

Verdict: 14/32 are negative after fees; none reach statistical significance (|t| > 2.3).

Layer B — 5 overlay sources (info-orthogonal)

To raise eff-N toward ≥5, we added 5 sources from different data: funding-carry, perp-basis, carry-vol, cross-asset lead/lag, social sentiment. These are deliberately telemetry-only (their units ≠ price-return edge).

Validate-first (today, 735k rows): 0 / 5 predict returns. So they stay unwired — wiring them would import phantom edge. OFI (order-flow) still needs real-tape R² validation.

Verdict: the orthogonality instinct is right; the candidates just don't validate yet.

Sizing & gates — what actually controls risk

The sizer (computeSize)

A principled fractional-Kelly sizer with CVaR/vol/drawdown throttles, fail-closed (no edge → 0 size). It was just wired into the crypto path but is DISARMED shadow — the paper engine still sizes by conviction until the owner arms it.

Phantom-edge finding (live shadow)
A strong ensemble vote (net≈0.8) produced a ~$6–7k "priced" size, because the sizer currently reads signal intensity as a return-edge. DISARMED = zero harm; it confirmed both sizer inputs (edge + win-probability) must be calibrated from real outcomes before arming.

The gates

Energy gate (computeU): advisory. It records ΔU but does not block trades (DISARMED). It is not what is holding back profits.

Real skip-gates: fee-hurdle (edge < round-trip fee → skip), multi-horizon (move must clear 0.15% rt), hysteresis (cool-down after exits). These reduce trade count — which is correct behavior on a no-edge signal (you do not want more trades on a signal that doesn't work).

The bottleneck is edge, not gating.

What is genuinely missing (path to real edge)

1. Fix the reward:risk first (highest leverage, lowest effort)
The 0.08:1 realized RR is a structural defect independent of the signal. Winners are exited at ~$1, losers at ~$13. Even a random signal breaks even at symmetric RR. This is the single biggest unlock and it is a parameters/logic fix, not a research problem.
2. A factor that actually validates
No current factor clears |t|>2.3 / DSR. Options: (a) microstructure (OFI on real L2 tape — needs R² validation), (b) a calibrated signal→return-edge transform (so the sizer sizes on real edge, not intensity), (c) event/funding edges at longer horizons. Until one validates, arming the sizer is premature.
3. Calibrate the sizer inputs
Both edgeMean and winProb must come from walk-forward outcomes (AFL_LEDGER) before the DISARMED sizer is armed. The infrastructure exists (factorCalibration); it needs an edge to calibrate on.

Open questions for the reviewer

  • Stop / target placement: we set take = 2× stop from a volatility estimate. Is that sane for 1-minute crypto perp, or is the stop too tight (noise stop-outs) / target too tight (cutting winners)?
  • Short bias: the ensemble calls short 88% of the time. Is that a signal-construction bug, or is it reading a real regime? How would you diagnose systematic directional bias?
  • Position sizing at $10–15k/trade: we cap at 10% equity per position (paper). For real size, what Kelly fraction / max-gross / correlation haircut would you run across BTC/ETH/SOL/SUI/WIF/AVAX?
  • Edge sources: for a discretionary prop trader, which of {order-flow, funding-carry, lead/lag, sentiment, event} actually pays at intraday crypto horizon? Where would you look first?
  • Time horizon: is 1-minute even the right frame for a human-reviewable crypto book, or should the edge live on 15m/1h where noise is smaller?
