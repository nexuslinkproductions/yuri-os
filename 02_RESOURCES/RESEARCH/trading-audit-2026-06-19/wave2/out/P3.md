# PEER 3 — Fleet Topology Critique & Refinement (GLM-5.2)

## 1. PRO-TOPOLOGY VERDICT

**Verdict: Your latency-budget partition is correct in principle, but the IPC substrate and the execution/OMS seam are under-specified. One structural divergence from industry norm is material.**

### Where your partition MATCHES industry norm

The four-tier split (market-data/exec hot path → risk → alpha → post-trade learning) is the canonical **process-isolation-by-latency-budget** pattern. Two primary sources establish this as the professional default:

1. **LMAX Disruptor engineering paper + Martin Thompson's Mechanical Sympathy blog** — LMAX built the reference design for separating the hot "business-logic processor" (single-threaded, deterministic) from the input-disruptor (journaling/marshalling) and output-disruptor (replication), communicating via lock-free ring buffers. The load-bearing claim: *marshal the wire once into a ring, let a single thread consume deterministically, fan results out on another ring*. This is the architecture your A1↔A2↔A3 hot path should follow.
   - LMAX Disruptor docs: https://lmax-exchange.github.io/disruptor/
   - Mechanical Sympathy (Martin Thompson): https://mechanical-sympathy.blogspot.com/2011/07/memory-barriers.html — *"keep the business logic in one thread; let the disruptor handle the concurrency at the seams"*; Thompson explicitly warns against sharing a thread between journaling (latency-tolerant) and matching (latency-critical) — which is exactly what the current monolithic `orchestrator.mjs` does.

2. **Aeron (Real Logic, Martin Thompson + Todd Montgomery)** — the open-source messaging system designed for low-latency inter-process pub/sub on a single host (shared-memory / IPC transport) and across hosts (UDP). It is the canonical reference for "in-process ring buffer for the hot path, durable log for the cold path." For a single M2-Pro machine the IPC/shared-media transport is the relevant mode.
   - https://github.com/real-logic/aeron — *"efficient unicast and multicast message transmission... suitable for high-frequency trading"*

**The slow-brain/fast-hands consensus** (your A3/A4 slow, A1/A2 fast) is independently corroborated by the academic multi-agent literature: the sibling note `05-multiagent-desk-llm-latency.md` confirms no LLM can be on the sub-second execution path (200ms–2s inference floor), and every viable system (TradingAgents arXiv:2412.20138, QuantAgent arXiv:2509.09995, CrossTrade) decouples slow deliberation from a deterministic fast executor reading a shared thesis store. Your partition inherits this correctly.

### Where your partition DIVERGES from industry norm (material)

**D1 — Risk must be a CROSSED CHECK, not just a sibling agent.** In real desks risk is *not* a single peer agent that competes for the bus; it is a **hard cross-check enforced at the order-egress seam**. The classic pattern (every serious OMS): the strategy produces an intent, risk signs it (or vetoes it) at the gateway, the executor only fires risk-cleared intents. Your design puts A2 as a *reactive* kill sentinel (it watches positions and flattens on breach) — that is the **drawdown breaker** role, which is necessary but *insufficient*. You are missing the **pre-trade risk gate**: A3's sized signal must pass through A2's veto *before* A1 quotes on it. As written, A1 could quote on a signal that breaches gross-exposure the instant it lands. Given the pre-fed risk reality (`maxGrossExposurePct=6.0` = 600%, crypto path bypasses `computeSize`, eff-N=1.15 at ρ=0.8), this is not a theoretical gap — the current code already lets risk be bypassed.

**Recommendation:** A2 has *two* sub-roles with different latency budgets:
- **A2-pre (gate):** synchronous veto on every sized signal before it reaches A1's quote engine. ≤1 cycle. Must be cheap (a position/exposure check, not a model).
- **A2-post (sentinel):** the reactive drawdown/kill breaker you already have. Sub-tick.

**D2 — You have no OMS / drop-copy.** The named firm pattern is strategy → risk → execution → **OMS** (state of every order, partial fills, cancels) → **drop-copy** (independent audit copy of every fill). You folded OMS into A1 (fill-detect) and have no drop-copy at all. On a paper system this is tolerable; on a live system the lack of an independent fill-audit trail is a regulatory and debugging liability. **Flag as missing** (Section "WHAT WE ARE MISSING").

**D3 — MM (A1) inside every agent? No.** Risk as a *cross-check* belongs at the egress seam (D1), but risk *logic* duplicated into every agent is an anti-pattern (Thompson, Disruptor paper: one authoritative owner per state shard; duplicated risk state drifts). A2 owns risk state authoritatively; A1 owns quote state; A3 owns signal state. The kill invariant is enforced by the *bus contract*, not by each agent re-deriving risk.

### Summary verdict
- ✅ Latency-budget partition: industry-correct (LMAX/Aeron primary sources).
- ✅ Slow/fast split: consensus-correct.
- ⚠️ Risk is reactive-only — missing the pre-trade gate at the egress seam.
- ⚠️ OMS collapsed into A1, no drop-copy.
- ⚠️ "SQLite bus + pub/sub ring" is the right *idea* but the wrong *substrate* for the hot path (see §3).

## 3. INTER-AGENT CONTRACT

### Verdict: **REPLACE** "SQLite bus + pub/sub ring" for the hot path. Keep SQLite as the cold durable ledger.

**Why SQLite is wrong for the hot path (A1↔A2↔A3):** SQLite is a disk-backed B-tree with a global write lock (WAL helps but doesn't eliminate contention). Every `INSERT` is a syscall + fsync-adjacent cost (even WAL: ~0.1–1ms per write under contention). At ≤1s cycle / ≤200ms quote budget, the bus must be **sub-millisecond, allocation-free, lock-free**. SQLite is the *drop-copy / audit / cold ledger*, never the hot bus. This is exactly the LMAX lesson: the input/output disruptors are in-memory ring buffers; the journal (the durable record) is a separate append concern that does *not* gate the hot path.

**What to use on M2-Pro single-machine:**

**Tier 1 (hot path, A1↔A2↔A3): lock-free SPSC/MPSC ring buffer in shared memory OR Node.js in-process if co-located.**

Two viable options for Node.js on M2-Pro:

- **Option A — In-process ring (if A1+A2 live in one Node process, threads separated via `worker_threads`):** A `SharedArrayBuffer`-backed ring with `Atomics.wait`/`notify` for the producer-consumer handshake. This is the closest JS analogue to the LMAX Disruptor (Thompson's "single-writer principle" per ring slot). Latency: nanoseconds–microseconds. **This is the recommended hot path** because the M2-Pro has no co-location benefit to justify a second process for A1+A2, and shared-memory IPC between *processes* in Node (Unix domain sockets / `node-ipc`) adds serialization tax (~10–50µs/msg) that `SharedArrayBuffer` avoids.
- **Option B — Shared-memory IPC between processes (Aeron-style):** Unix domain socket or a memory-mapped file. Use only if you want process-level isolation (A2 in a separate process so an A1 crash can't corrupt A2's risk state). The cost: every message crosses a serialization + syscall boundary. On M2-Pro at 3 symbols × 15 msg/s ≈ 45 msg/s, this is *fine* — the rate is trivial. At the 10–30 symbol ceiling it's still fine (≤450 msg/s). **Process isolation is worth the serialization tax for A2 specifically**, because A2's correctness must not depend on A1's health.

**Recommendation: hybrid.**
- A1 + A3 in the same Node process (or two processes with `SharedArrayBuffer`/Unix-domain-socket ring) — they are the latency-critical, mutually-trusting pair.
- **A2 in a separate process** communicating via Unix domain socket (Aeron-style, but trivially: a length-prefixed JSON or flatbuffer frame over a socket). Process isolation means an A1/A3 crash leaves A2 alive to flatten.
- A4/A5/A6/A7 read/write **SQLite** (the existing `afl-prediction-ledger` pattern) — they are latency-tolerant and benefit from durability + queryability.

### The A2-kill invariant (crisp)

> **INVARIANT:** From the moment any position's exposure breaches a hard limit (gross > maxGross, drawdown > floor, VPIN > toxicity threshold), A2 emits a FLAT/KILL order that reaches the execution surface (A1's quote-engine ingress or a direct cancel path) within **one cycle** (≤ the slowest hot-path tick, target ≤1s).
>
> **Bus guarantee:** A2's KILL is published on the **highest-priority ring slot** (discrete priority lane, not sharing the A3→A1 signal slot). A1's ingress *must drain the KILL slot before quoting* on every tick (a single `Atomics.load` check — nanoseconds). If A2's process is dead, A1 **fails closed**: no A2 heartbeat for N ms → A1 auto-flattens and stops quoting. (This is the "risk down = trading down" rule every desk enforces.)

The fail-closed heartbeat is what makes the invariant robust to A2's own failure — a single point of risk authority that *can* die safely because its absence is treated as a kill signal.

## 4. FLEET SIZE

**Answer: 5 agents for the minimal-viable production target. 7 for the extended measurer build.**

Not 4, not 7 — **5** is the defensible minimum. Reasoning:

**Why 4 is too few:** A1 (MM) and A3 (alpha) *cannot* share a daemon. The pre-fed cycle reality is the proof: the sequential 6-market for-loop takes ~9s and the alpha compute is the bulk of it. If A1 and A3 share a process, A1's requote latency is gated by A3's 9s cycle — which defeats the entire point of splitting the hot path. **A1 must own its event loop exclusively.** So the floor is A1, A2, A3, A4 = 4 *only if* A1 is already separate — but the ground truth says it isn't. The *split itself* is the 5th unit of work (extracting A1 out). Once extracted: **A1, A2, A3, A4, + the bus/supervisor** = 4 agents + 1 infrastructure layer. If we don't count the bus as an agent, then 4 agents is right *but only after the A1 extraction*. I'll call it **4 agents + mandatory A1/A2 process separation**, and the supervisor is non-optional infrastructure (not a 5th "agent" but a required layer — see §5).

**Why 7 is fine but not yet:** A5–A7 are measurer/advisory roles. On M2-Pro they do **not** contend on the hot path because:
- A5 (fill-sim) is per-cycle, same budget as A3 — but it can ride A3's cycle or run as a post-cycle beat without touching the A1↔A2 ring.
- A6 (news, GLM-native, tmux lane) is advisory-only, minutes cadence, zero hot-path contention.
- A7 (funding harvester) is a beat, minutes cadence, disarmed measurer.

**M2-Pro capacity check:** 6P + 4E cores. Hot-path agents (A1, A2) pin to 2 P-cores. A3 uses 1–2 P-cores for the parallel `Promise.allSettled`. A4/A5 are bursty, land on E-cores or idle P-cores. A6/A7 are I/O-bound, E-cores. **7 agents do not saturate the hot path** — the binding constraint is network RTT (50–100ms to Binance AWS), not CPU. At 3 symbols the CPU is ~idle. The 10–30 symbol ceiling is network-bound, not agent-count-bound.

**So: build to 4 (extract A1, separate A2, demote A4 to a beat), then add A5/A6/A7 as the engine proves out. 7 is the target architecture; 4 (+ supervisor + bus) is the first ship.**

## 5. SUPERVISION

**What does NOT exist today (must be built):** a process supervisor / watchdog layer.

**Recommendation: launchd with `KeepAlive` + a lightweight watchdog agent.**

| Layer | Mechanism | Owns | Exists? |
|-------|-----------|------|---------|
| **launchd plist per agent** | `KeepAlive=true` (respawn on exit), `RunAtLoad=true`, `Nice`/`QoS` pinning (P-core for A1/A2), `ThrottleInterval=10` (anti-flap) | restart-on-crash, boot-time start, CPU/QoS class | **NO** — current monolith is one hand-started daemon |
| **Watchdog (A0- OverSeer)** | a tiny process that heartbeats A1/A2/A3 every N ms; if A2 heartbeat missing → force A1 flatten; if A1 missing → A2 flattens; writes a supervision log | cross-agent liveness, fail-closed orchestration, escalation | **NO** |
| **Bus liveness** | the ring itself carries heartbeats (A2 publishes a `HEARTBEAT` slot every tick; A1 treats missing heartbeat as kill) | implicit in the bus contract (§3) | **NO** |

**Why launchd, not PM2/systemd/custom:** launchd is the native M2-Pro supervisor, handles KeepAlive + QoS pinning + boot-start natively, no extra dependency. PM2 is fine but redundant on macOS. A custom watchdog is *also* needed because launchd only knows "process alive/dead" — it cannot enforce the **fail-closed cross-agent invariant** (A2 down → A1 must flatten, not just "A2 restarts"). That cross-agent logic needs the A0-overseer or the bus-heartbeat contract.

**Supervision hierarchy:**
```
launchd (KeepAlive per agent)
  ├── A1 (daemon, P-core)
  ├── A2 (daemon, P-core, isolated)
  ├── A3 (daemon)
  ├── A4 (beat, interval)
  ├── A0-watchdog (daemon) ← enforces fail-closed invariant
  └── [A5/A6/A7 when armed]
```
