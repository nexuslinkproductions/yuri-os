## FUNCTIONAL LEADERSHIP ARCHETYPES

### L1 — Research-Vision CEO
**Function:** Embodies research ethos as company direction; translates collective-intelligence philosophy into product/investment/talent narrative; external face for the lab's scientific identity.
**Capabilities:**
- Synthetic intelligence research vision (complex systems, collective intelligence, bio-inspired AI)
- Cross-domain narrative fluency (connects nature, evolution, AI theory, and societal mission)
- Founder credibility that attracts world-class research talent
- Research community positioning and conference presence
- Long-horizon mission-holding while managing short-term investor dynamics

### L2 — Frontier-Research CTO
**Function:** Technical authority and research quality bar; holds the frontier engineering standard; co-designs the methods that define the lab's identity.
**Capabilities:**
- Deep expertise in core architecture (e.g., Transformer-class innovations)
- Judgment on what constitutes genuinely frontier vs. incremental research
- Sets the internal technical bar and evaluates research candidates
- Bridges theory and engineering feasibility
- Drives technical roadmap continuity across research cycles

### L3 — Operations & Sovereign-Strategy COO/Chairman
**Function:** Converts research capability into business reality; manages Japan-specific regulatory, diplomatic, and enterprise relationships; holds operational continuity so researchers don't context-switch.
**Capabilities:**
- Government relations and regulatory navigation (Japan MFA-tier fluency)
- Enterprise partnership origination (banking, defense, infrastructure sectors)
- Capital strategy and investor relations
- Cross-cultural business translation (Japan ↔ global tech)
- Legal and compliance oversight (corporate formation, M&A readiness, IPO preparedness)

## RESEARCH ARCHETYPES

### R1 — Frontier Research Scientist
**Function:** Core hypothesis generator; pushes against the edges of what's known; pursues depth over breadth; the primary value-creation unit of the lab.
**Capabilities:**
- Hypothesis-first experimental design (isolate riskiest assumption, minimum viable experiment)
- Novel algorithm or method conception (not benchmark optimization)
- Iterative refinement with persistence through repeated failures
- Cross-domain transfer (biology, evolution, physics → ML mechanisms)
- Clear scientific communication: distill dense results into replicable papers

### R2 — Evolutionary-Methods Researcher
**Function:** Specializes in nature-inspired optimization — evolutionary algorithms, genetic programming, sparsity, model merging; translates biological principles into ML training dynamics.
**Capabilities:**
- Evolutionary algorithm design (genetic search, mutation operators, selection pressure)
- Model-merging and compositionality techniques (creating new models from existing ones)
- Compute-efficient experimentation (idea leverage over raw scale)
- Population-based and distributed search methods
- Benchmark design for open-ended / non-saturating evaluation

### R3 — Collective Intelligence / Multi-Agent Researcher
**Function:** Models emergent behavior from interacting agents; designs swarm and ensemble systems; researches how coordinated small models outperform monolithic ones.
**Capabilities:**
- Multi-agent system theory and simulation
- Orchestration protocols for LLM ensembles (debate, voting, specialization)
- Collective behavior emergence from simple local rules
- Evaluation frameworks for multi-agent outcomes vs. single-model baselines
- Communication and coordination protocol design

### R4 — Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) Researcher
**Function:** Works in the RSI Lab; goal is to make AI redesign its own training and development process; highest-risk / highest-upside research vertical.
**Capabilities:**
- Meta-learning and self-modification architectures
- Automated code generation and self-rewriting agent systems
- Adversarial coevolution and digital red-team dynamics
- Open-ended learning theory (non-saturating environments, novelty search)
- Safety-aware RSI design (preventing runaway self-modification)

### R5 — Applied Research Scientist (Domain)
**Function:** Takes frontier methods and drives them into a specific high-value domain (finance, defense, language/culture, healthcare); translates abstract techniques into domain-shaped proofs-of-concept.
**Capabilities:**
- Domain-specific knowledge (finance, defense, biomedical, or industrial)
- Rapid prototype-to-demo pipeline with industry partners
- Evaluation design grounded in domain KPIs, not just ML metrics
- Partner-facing research communication
- Distillation of enterprise problems into tractable research formulations

### R6 — Japanese Language / Culture AI Specialist
**Function:** Ensures AI systems perform correctly and culturally coherently in Japanese; a Japan-sovereign-AI necessity that most non-Japan labs lack.
**Capabilities:**
- Japanese NLP, morphology, and writing-system handling
- Cultural nuance in model evaluation and RLHF data curation
- Benchmark design for Japanese language tasks
- Bilingual research dissemination (EN ↔ JA)
- Localization of global AI research findings for Japan-specific deployment

## ENGINEERING ARCHETYPES

### E1 — Advanced Core Engineer (Research Infrastructure)
**Function:** Builds the substrate on which research runs — distributed training, experiment tracking, evaluation pipelines; converts research code into reproducible, scalable systems.
**Capabilities:**
- Distributed computing and large-scale training optimization (GPU cluster management)
- Data pipeline design: ingestion, preprocessing, versioning at scale
- Benchmarking infrastructure and automated evaluation harnesses
- Experiment reproducibility tooling
- Low-level systems optimization (CUDA, memory efficiency, throughput)

### E2 — Applied Research Engineer (Product Bridge)
**Function:** Takes research outputs and productizes them — builds demos, APIs, and agent systems that make research accessible; hybrid of engineer and researcher.
**Capabilities:**
- Rapid prototyping of novel AI capabilities into user-testable forms
- Multi-agent and autonomous system integration
- API design for AI capabilities (reliability, latency, versioning)
- Evaluation-in-product instrumentation (real-world vs. lab performance)
- Cross-functional: speaks fluent research AND product

### E3 — Full-Stack Product Engineer
**Function:** Builds user-facing AI products end-to-end; owns the experience layer of research-turned-product (Sakana Chat, Marlin, Fugu class).
**Capabilities:**
- Full-stack web/app development with AI integration
- Rapid iteration and A/B testing culture
- Real-time AI inference integration and latency optimization
- Product analytics and user behavior instrumentation
- AI-native UX patterns (streaming output, uncertainty display, multi-turn)

### E4 — Cybersecurity Engineer
**Function:** Secures enterprise AI deployments; specifically relevant given Sakana's defense, intelligence, and banking sector clients where security posture is non-negotiable.
**Capabilities:**
- Security architecture for AI systems (adversarial robustness, data isolation)
- Penetration testing and red-teaming of AI applications
- Compliance frameworks for regulated industries (finance, defense, government)
- Digital coevolution / automated red-team research (aligns with RSI Lab "Digital Red Queen")
- Enterprise key management, access control, audit logging

## OPERATING MODEL PRINCIPLES (lean high-capacity lab → agent governance)

### OP-1: IDEAS OVER COMPUTE
The primary competitive edge is intellectual leverage — novel methods that achieve
frontier results without requiring frontier compute budgets. For agent governance:
agents should be evaluated on the quality of their reasoning path, not raw throughput.
Prefer agents that solve problems differently from existing approaches over agents that
brute-force with more tokens.

### OP-2: UNDERSTANDING OVER IMPLEMENTATION
Sakana's hiring test is: "can you explain why your design works?" not "can you produce
working code fast?" For agent governance: an agent that cannot articulate the mechanism
behind its output is not trusted. Every agent should emit a reasoning trace or
confidence annotation, not just a result.

### OP-3: COLLECTIVE INTELLIGENCE BEATS MONOLITHIC SCALE
A coordinated ensemble of smaller, specialized agents outperforms one large generalist.
The lab's entire product suite (Fugu, Marlin) is built on this premise. For governance:
prefer swarm/ensemble architectures with specialization contracts over monolithic
super-agents. Each archetype should be narrowly excellent, not broadly mediocre.

### OP-4: FLAT HIERARCHY, DEEP AUTONOMY
~20 people built frontier research products in 12 months. This requires radically high
autonomy at the individual contributor level — minimal approval chains. For agent
governance: agents should be self-governing within a defined capability envelope. The
governance layer sets scope + safety rails, then steps back. Over-coordination kills
throughput.

### OP-5: Japan-SOVEREIGN, GLOBALLY-LEGIBLE
Sakana operates in Japanese enterprise culture (long relationships, nemawashi, consensus)
while publishing globally legible research (NeurIPS, Nature). For agent governance:
agents should carry both a domain-native context (local knowledge, norms) and a
generalist communication surface (can explain outputs to any peer lane). No agent
should be domain-siloed.

### OP-6: EVOLUTIONARY SELF-IMPROVEMENT IS THE LONG GAME
The RSI Lab is Sakana's bet that the AI development process itself should be automated
and self-improving. For agent governance: build agents that can be updated by their
own outputs (self-improving loop), with safety gates on what can be self-modified vs.
what requires human approval. Recursive improvement is the destination, not a feature.
